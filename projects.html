CardioCare Quest Lead

I am the lead researcher on a community playtesting study of CardioCare Quest, a playful telehealth intervention for hypertension management designed with and for Navajo, Black, and Hispanic communities, supported by NIH funding awarded to the Playful Health Technology Lab. The study runs with adults who have intellectual and developmental disabilities at the Hozhoni Foundation in Flagstaff, Arizona, a population historically under-served by digital health research.

I characterize participants' cardiovascular risk profiles, how they engage during open community play, and how their hypertension knowledge and self-monitoring attitudes shift afterward. I coordinate the undergraduate capstone team building Twine-based mini-game prototypes, work with a designer on the clinician-facing Figma dashboard, and contribute to protocol design, consent procedures, and accessibility accommodations.

Beyond the study itself, the work generates insights on how playful health technology can meaningfully engage adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities, and it feeds the lab's NIH-funded program on hypertension management in Indigenous and under-served communities. It anchors a first-author manuscript in preparation.

Robomoji Contributor

A study of how disabled users engage an assistive robotic arm across playful and functional uses, reimagining the robot as a tool for creative expression and identity rather than only a means of completing activities of daily living. We argue that authorship, creativity, and play deserve a place alongside task performance in assistive robotics.

Twenty-four participants from a disability service organization controlled a Kinova Gen3 arm through four interfaces spanning different levels of abstraction, and took part in a robot-puppet workshop imagining the robot in their own lives. A thematic analysis of interviews, input logs, and observations surfaced visions for creative, playful, and social robot use, which we translate into a speculative design for more expressive assistive interfaces. I contribute to the interaction design and user-study methodology for users with upper-limb mobility impairments.

Contributor · Assistive robotics · ACM DIS 2027 · with Mavis Murdock, Parth Kheni, Elaine Short & Jared Duval

SpokeIt Contributor

SpokeIt is an NIH-supported mobile speech-therapy game for children born with orofacial cleft that turns at-home articulation practice into playful, motivating exercises between clinical visits.

I contributed the player-registration experience: the mobile onboarding flow through which a new player creates a profile before gameplay. I aligned this flow with the research team's consent and demographic-collection requirements, then tested and iterated on its usability with the team. I was a member of the 2024 SpokeIt team that won First Prize at the NAU Engineering Fest for advancing accessible and engaging speech therapy.

Contributor · Serious game · Speech therapy · Mobile · NIH-supported · 1st Prize, NAU Engineering Fest 2024

Interactive Data Visualization Lead

As lead developer, I built a web platform that turns a systematic review of 215 peer-reviewed accessibility and serious-games papers (1994–2023) into an interactive, accessible design-space explorer across nine experiential design dimensions, so researchers can navigate three decades of work quickly and equitably.

Using HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and D3.js, I implemented coordinated spectrum heatmaps, dot matrices, and filtering views, and built full keyboard operability, screen-reader compatibility, and audio-tactile sonification from the start, meeting WCAG 2.1 AA contrast. In a pilot expert evaluation with eleven HCI researchers, nine preferred the heatmap view over conventional charts and completed synthesis tasks in roughly half the time of comparable manual review. The platform anchors a first-author journal manuscript in preparation.

NetGauge Contributor

NetGauge is a platform of co-created games that make measuring broadband quality enjoyable and feed crowdsourced broadband coverage maps, addressing a driver of rural and tribal health disparities.

I contribute to Rapid Iterative Testing and Evaluation (RITE) sessions, in which a participant uses the platform until they hit a usability problem, at which point the team iterates on the build before the next participant is run. I facilitate sessions and capture usability problems in session notes, support rapid iteration on the platform frontend between sessions, and contribute to the accessibility review of the games in the suite.
