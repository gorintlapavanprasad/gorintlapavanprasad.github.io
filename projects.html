CardioCare Quest Currently building

A culturally grounded, accessible serious game for hypertension management, co-designed with and for the Navajo community. Built together with the Hozhoni Foundation in Flagstaff, Arizona, it is made for adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities and pairs playful activities, such as a Dog Walking game, with blood-pressure, step-count, and heart-rate measures so that self-management feels motivating rather than clinical.

The work spans two pieces: a community co-design workshop where participants shaped the game, and an open community playtest that looked at how people engaged and what shifted in their hypertension knowledge and self-monitoring. It anchors my dissertation on playful telehealth for Indigenous communities.

Robomoji

A study of how disabled users engage an assistive robotic arm across playful and functional uses, reimagining the robot as a tool for creative expression and identity, not just activities of daily living. We argue that authorship, creativity, and play deserve a place alongside task performance in assistive robotics.

Twenty-four participants from a disability service organization controlled a Kinova Gen3 arm through four interfaces spanning different levels of abstraction, and took part in a robot-puppet workshop imagining the robot in their own lives. A thematic analysis of interviews, input logs, and observations surfaced visions for creative, playful, and social robot use, which we translate into a speculative design for more expressive assistive interfaces.

Assistive robotics · HCI · ACM DIS 2027 · with Mavis Murdock, Parth Kheni, Elaine Short & Jared Duval

SpokeIt

A mobile speech-therapy game that turns at-home articulation practice into motivating, playful exercises for children, including children born with cleft lip and palate. It listens to a child's speech and weaves practice into the story so that progress feels like play, helping kids keep up their exercises between clinical visits.

Serious game · Speech therapy · Mobile · Playful Health Technology Lab

Data Visualization

Interactive, accessible visualizations that map decades of accessibility and serious-games research so researchers can make sense of the field quickly and equitably. One platform organizes 215 peer-reviewed papers (1994–2023) along nine experiential design dimensions, pairing coordinated heatmaps and dot matrices with full keyboard operability, screen-reader support, audio-tactile sonification, and WCAG 2.1 AA contrast.

In a pilot study with eleven HCI researchers, most preferred the heatmap view over conventional charts and completed synthesis tasks in roughly half the time of manual review, suggesting that accessible visualization is a prerequisite for equitable knowledge synthesis, not an afterthought.

NetGauge

A location-based games platform that crowdsources broadband measurements, connecting people and place through play. By turning internet-speed data collection into a game, it gathers real-world coverage data, including in rural communities, while giving players a reason to explore and contribute.

The platform was co-designed so that data collection fits into everyday movement and local knowledge, exploring how play can power community-driven measurement of where connectivity does and does not reach.

Games platform · Co-design · Broadband · Playful Health Technology Lab
