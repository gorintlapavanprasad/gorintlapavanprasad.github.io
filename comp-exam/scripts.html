Slide 3What is hypertension?

130/80Hypertension is blood pressure that stays high: 130 over 80 or above.

silent killerNo symptoms, so it is called the silent killer, but over years it harms the heart, kidneys, and brain.

1.28B · US 45.6%1.28 billion adults have it worldwide, about 1 in 3 aged 30 to 79; in the US, 45.6 percent.

600M · 44% unawareAbout 600 million, 44 percent, do not know they have it.

10 mmHgBut it is manageable: a 10 mmHg drop in systolic cuts cardiovascular events about 20 percent, stroke 27, death 13.

Takeaway: Control comes from daily behavior, so I look at playful, everyday technology as daily support.

Slide 4For urban Navajo (Diné) adults, hypertension is under-diagnosed.

bars ≈ 28%Self-report sits near the US average.

50 of 97But measured studies run higher: in Percy's Navajo survey, only 50 of 97 with high blood pressure had ever been told.

4th cause of deathHeart disease was the 4th leading cause of death on the New Mexico Navajo Nation, 2021 to 2024.

urban Diné in FlagstaffThe people at the center of this work are urban Diné adults in Flagstaff.

Background

Slide 10Hypertension is managed through five daily behaviors.

five factorsHypertension is managed through five daily behaviors: diet, exercise, medication, measurements, education.

first-lineLifestyle change is first-line care; even a 10 mmHg drop lowers risk.

Takeaway: I chart the literature by these five factors.

Slide 11Hypertension · Risk — Weight and sitting raise the risk, and one form of sitting is play.

7× oddsWeight raises the risk most: 7 times the odds of hypertension with obesity, in 833 adults with disabilities. That sample is from Taiwan, so I use it as cross-population evidence.

seated gamingIn teenagers, seated gaming was the only sitting habit linked to higher blood pressure.

Percy, localLocally, Percy measured 780 Navajo adults: of the 97 with high blood pressure, only 50 had been told.

Takeaway: So design for movement during play, and help people learn their own numbers.

Slide 13Disability · Proxy Care — A caregiver can scaffold a player, or quietly stand in for them.

The first disability theme is proxy care.

14.4 → 2.7A caregiver can help: in Caro's study, the help needed dropped from 14.4 times in session one to 2.7 by session three.

substitutionBut inclusion can slide into substitution, the supporter speaking for the person. Sum names that risk.

BircaninBircanin spent two years co-designing with adults with severe intellectual disability using Active Support: little and often, choice stays with the person.

Takeaway: The supporter scaffolds; the player decides. Partial participation, like pressing the cuff button, is real participation.

Slide 14Disability · Accessible Protocols — Few studies use accessibility standards.

Second, accessible protocols.

7 of 25A 2026 review found only 7 of 25 co-design studies with people with intellectual disability used standards like Easy Read.

Spencer GonzálezSpencer González worked three years with a paid advisory panel: easy-read text, adult pictures, story-based activities, polished prototypes.

Takeaway: Easy Read materials, short sessions, familiar places, and fair pay.

Slide 15Disability · Technosolutionism — Most accessible-tech work still treats disability as a deficit to fix.

Third, technosolutionism: the belief a new technology will fix the problem.

13 of 162Hassan's review: of 162 game-accessibility studies, only 13 drew on disability-studies theory; the rest framed disability as a deficit.

SumSum names the drive that casts disabled people as problems to fix.

YmousYmous and seven disabled scholars describe the epistemic violence of being written up as burdens. Their title: I am just terrified of my future.

Takeaway: Confirm the problem is real for the community, and treat opting out as a legitimate choice.

Slide 16Disability · DREEM — Learn before asking people for their time.

The last disability theme is a method from our lab: DREEM.

learn firstLearn first, from media made by disabled creators, before spending people's access labor.

four stepsFour steps: discover media, read it closely, journal, aggregate.

Takeaway: For this work: start with media by Diné and disabled creators, then bring better questions to the community.

Slide 17Indigenous · Sovereignty — Data sovereignty is rarely addressed.

Now the Indigenous branch. First, sovereignty.

4 of 84In Vigil-Hayes's critical review, only 4 of 84 Indigenous health-technology studies engaged with who owns and controls community data.

historical traumaHistorical trauma, from colonization and harmful research, shapes trust.

MaarMaar, in DREAM-GLOBAL, shows how to build and keep the research relationship; 98 percent would recommend it.

Takeaway: Community control of data under the CARE principles, results shared back, and a community advisory board.

Slide 18Indigenous · Cultural Grounding — Programs work when they build on culture.

Second Indigenous theme: cultural grounding.

42.7 vs 21.2The hula trial with 263 Native Hawaiian adults: 42.7 percent reached a healthy blood pressure, against 21.2. It is the only randomized trial of a cultural movement practice for blood pressure.

the lessonThe lesson is the approach, not the dance.

ARORAARORA, here at NAU with Navajo and Hopi youth, let an advisory board set the design values.

Takeaway: Start from what the community values, and let the community decide what is shared.

Slide 20Serious Games and Play — Most health games are closed systems.

Slide 22Participatory, Community-Based Research — Few studies design with the community.

Second theory theme: participatory, community-based research.

13 studiesAbout 13 studies designed with the community: 7 Indigenous, 6 disability, and the two barely overlap.

HarringtonHarrington warns that design workshops themselves can be an affluent, privileged activity.

Takeaway: Pay people, value community assets, let the community set goals, and stay after the funding ends.

Slide 23Wicked Problems and Research through Design — A health game is a wicked problem.

Third: wicked problems and research through design. No paper used these words, so this is from theory.

wicked problemA health game is a wicked problem: no single definition, and answers are better or worse, not right or wrong.

learn by makingResearch through design answers that by learning through making.

Situated PlaySituated Play Design: food traditions from many cultures became starting points for play.

Takeaway: Build on the play people already do, and treat the platform as a probe to learn with, not just a product.
