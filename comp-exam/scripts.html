Slide 13Background · Hypertension · Risk — Extra weight and seated gaming raise blood pressure.2 clicks

So what raises the risk? Two studies point to the same answer.

1 card: Lin et al. 2012, Table 8Lin [lin] and colleagues checked health records of 833 adults with disabilities in Taiwan. This is their table. Compared with underweight adults, overweight and obese adults had about seven times the odds of hypertension. This was a snapshot at one point in time, so it shows a link, not proof that weight causes it.

2 card: Goldfield et al. 2011, Table 3Goldfield [GOLD-feeld] and colleagues asked 282 overweight teenagers in Canada about screen time. In their table, TV and computer time were not linked to blood pressure. Only video games, marked VG, were linked to higher blood pressure. Again, this is a link from one survey, not proof that gaming causes it. The authors say snacking or stress during play may explain it.

So for design: the game should ask people to move, not to sit for a long time.

Slide 22Background · Indigenous · Data Governance — CARE, FAIR, and OCAP: who decides about Indigenous data?5 clicks

How can Indigenous communities set rules for their data? Three frameworks help.

1 card: CARECARE, from Carroll and colleagues, has four principles: collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility, and ethics. It asks: who has the right to decide about this data, and who benefits?

2 card: FAIRFAIR asks a different question: how should data be managed so others can find and reuse it? Findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable.

3 card: OCAPOCAP [OH-cap] was made by First Nations in Canada: ownership, control, access, and possession.

4 CARE and OCAP first, FAIR secondThey work together. CARE and OCAP come first: the community decides if and how data is shared. FAIR comes second, only inside those limits. FAIR without CARE can go against a tribe's sovereignty.

5 → What this means for designWhat this means for design: the Diné community decides what is shared, then FAIR keeps the shared data usable. This links to RQ2, and to PRQ3 later.

Slide 26Background · Serious Games and Play — What is a serious game, and what is serious play?~30 sec4 clicks

First, what is a serious game?

1 Wattanasoontorn et al. 2013: two aims …Wattanasoontorn [waht-tah-nah-SOON-torn] and colleagues say a serious game has two goals: to be fun, and to teach a skill or improve health. They studied 108 health games.

2 panel: Serious games for health (closed)Duval [doo-VAL] describes two kinds. Closed games: the rules are fixed, and the game decides what counts as success. Players follow the path the designers set, like a set clinic scenario.

3 panel: Serious play for health (open)Open play: there is still a health goal, but players can shape the rules and fit the play into their daily life, starting from play they already do.
