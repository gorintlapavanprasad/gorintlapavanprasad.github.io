Slide 1Playful Health Technology for Hypertension Management at the Intersection of Indigeneity and Disability

Good morning, and thank you all for being here.

My comprehensive exam is a scoping review titled Playful Health Technology for Hypertension Management at the Intersection of Indigeneity and Disability.

In plain terms, I'm asking what we know about games and playful technology that help people manage high blood pressure, and how much of that work reaches people who are both Indigenous and living with a disability.

I'd like to thank my committee: Dr. Duval, my chair, Dr. Amresh, Dr. Steinmacher, and Dr. Turmo Vidal.

Slide 2Land Acknowledgement

Before I begin, I want to acknowledge the land we are on.

Northern Arizona University sits at the base of the San Francisco Peaks, Dook'o'oosłííd, on homelands sacred to Native peoples of this region. We honor their past, present, and future generations, who have lived here for millennia and will always call this place home.

For this work, this is more than a formality. The people at the center of this review are Native people living here in Flagstaff, on this land.

Slide 3What is hypertension?5 clicks

So, what is hypertension?

1 Blood pressure that stays high, 130 over …It is blood pressure that stays at 130 over 80 or higher.

2 It usually has no symptoms, yet over …It usually has no symptoms, which is why it is called the silent killer, but over the years it damages the heart, kidneys, and brain.

3 1.28 billion adults live with hypertension worldwide, …And it is far more common than most people realize. Around the world, 1.28 billion adults live with hypertension, one in three adults aged 30 to 79. Here in the United States, that figure rises to 45.6 percent of adults.

4 An estimated 600 million adults with hypertension, …But what makes it truly dangerous is its silence. Of those 1.28 billion people, 600 million, 44 percent, have no idea they have it.

5 It is manageable: a 10 mmHg drop …The good news is that it can be managed. A 10-point drop in systolic pressure cuts major cardiac events by about 20 percent and stroke by 27 percent. Most of that control comes from everyday behavior.
