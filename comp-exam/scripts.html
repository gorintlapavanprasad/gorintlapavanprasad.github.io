Slide 1Playful Health Technology for Hypertension Management at the Intersection of Indigeneity and Disability

Good morning, and thank you all for being here.

My comprehensive exam is a scoping review titled Playful Health Technology for Hypertension Management at the Intersection of Indigeneity and Disability.

In plain terms, I'm asking what we know about games and playful technology that help people manage high blood pressure, and how much of that work reaches people who are both Indigenous and living with a disability.

I'd like to thank my committee: Dr. Duval, my chair, Dr. Amresh, Dr. Steinmacher, and Dr. Turmo Vidal.

2 It usually has no symptoms, yet over …It usually has no symptoms, which is why it is called the silent killer, but over the years it damages the heart, kidneys, and brain.

Slide 24Background · Serious Games and Play — What is a serious game, and what is serious play?~30 sec4 clicks

Before the hypertension games, a quick word on what a serious game is.

1 Wattanasoontorn et al. 2013: two aims …Wattanasoontorn and colleagues define a serious game as one with two aims: to entertain, and to build a skill or improve health. Their survey of 108 health games sorted them by purpose, by player, and by stage of disease.

2 panel: Serious games for health (closed)Duval separates two forms. Serious games for health are closed systems with rigid rules, where the technology mediates the whole experience.

3 panel: Serious play for health (open)Serious play for health is aimed at a health outcome but left open, so people can shape it to their own lives, starting from the play they already do, as in Altarriba Bertran's situated play design.

4 → Next: which form do hypertension games take?So the question for hypertension is: which form do these games take, and how well do they work?

Slide 46Backup · Blood Pressure Categories — The blood-pressure categories.

categoriesNormal below 120 and 80; Elevated 120 to 129; Stage 1 is 130 to 139 or 80 to 89; Stage 2 is 140 or 90 and above.

the line movedMoving the line from 140/90 to 130/80 raised US prevalence from about 32 to 46 percent, 31 million more adults. WHO still uses 140/90.
